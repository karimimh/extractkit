Here are **solo-developer SaaS ideas across distinct fields**, biased toward products with recurring revenue, narrow users, and clear monthly pain.

A good solo SaaS angle right now is: **small business + annoying recurring workflow + compliance/admin/automation**. Small businesses are already adopting AI tools rapidly, with the U.S. Chamber reporting 58% generative AI adoption among small businesses in 2025. ([U.S. Chamber of Commerce][1]) In the UK, late payments remain a strong pain point, with FSB reporting that 63% of businesses spend time chasing overdue payments. ([FSB][2]) Accessibility is also becoming more commercially relevant because the European Accessibility Act came into effect on 28 June 2025. ([AccessibleEU][3])

## The best 7 for a solo web developer

### 1. Client Payment Chaser

**Best customer:** freelancers, agencies, consultants.
**MVP:** connect invoices manually or via Stripe/Xero/QuickBooks later; send reminders; show overdue dashboard; generate escalation emails.
**Pricing:** £9–£29/month.
**Why it is good:** painful, recurring, easy to explain, clear ROI.

### 2. Accessibility Monitor for Small Websites

**Best customer:** web agencies and small e-commerce stores.
**MVP:** crawl 5–50 pages, run automated checks, produce plain-English fixes, export branded reports.
**Pricing:** £19–£99/month depending on page count.
**Why it is good:** agencies can resell it to clients, and compliance pressure creates urgency.

### 3. Quote Follow-Up CRM for Trades

**Best customer:** tradespeople who quote jobs but forget to follow up.
**MVP:** add lead, quote amount, follow-up schedule, SMS/email templates, win/loss tracking.
**Pricing:** £15–£49/month.
**Why it is good:** one recovered job can pay for the tool for months.

### 4. Tutor Operations Hub

**Best customer:** independent tutors and small tutoring businesses.
**MVP:** student list, lesson notes, homework links, parent updates, payment reminders.
**Pricing:** £10–£40/month.
**Why it is good:** tutors have recurring students, recurring admin, and simple workflows.

### 5. Menu Profit Tracker

**Best customer:** cafés, food trucks, small restaurants.
**MVP:** ingredients, recipes, dish cost, recommended price, margin alerts.
**Pricing:** £19–£59/month.
**Why it is good:** food costs change often, and owners care about margins.

### 6. Sponsor Pipeline Manager for Creators

**Best customer:** newsletter writers, podcasters, YouTubers.
**MVP:** sponsor CRM, deal stages, deliverables, invoice dates, renewal reminders.
**Pricing:** £12–£49/month.
**Why it is good:** creators are becoming businesses but often run sponsorships in spreadsheets.

### 7. Subcontractor Compliance Tracker

**Best customer:** small construction companies.
**MVP:** subcontractor profiles, document uploads, expiry reminders, “approved/not approved” status.
**Pricing:** £39–£149/month.
**Why it is good:** boring, important, recurring, and less likely to be attacked by trendy AI startups.

## Most realistic first product

I would start with **Client Payment Chaser** or **Accessibility Monitor**.

**Client Payment Chaser** is easier to sell because the value is direct: “get paid faster.”
**Accessibility Monitor** is better if you already know web development well because you can use your skills to build the scanner, reports, and agency workflow.

## Simple validation test

Before building the full SaaS, make a landing page for one idea with:

**“I help [specific user] solve [pain] without [annoying manual task].”**

Examples:

> I help freelance developers chase overdue invoices without awkward emails.

> I help small web agencies send monthly accessibility reports to clients without manual audits.

Then message 50 people in that niche. Do not ask, “Would you use this?” Ask:

> How do you handle this today?

> How often does this happen?

> What does it cost you when it goes wrong?

> Would you pay £19/month if this saved you 2 hours or one lost payment?

My strongest recommendation: **build something narrow, ugly-useful, and business-facing**. A boring tool that saves money every month beats a beautiful app that people only use once.
